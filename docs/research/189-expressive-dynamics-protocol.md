# Expressive dynamics and phrase legibility

[Issue #189](https://github.com/rhythmatician/FractalSync/issues/189) asks which dynamics listeners hear in music and see in Julia motion. The first comparison tests whether a few descriptors explain perceived expression after accounting for activity and appearance. A separate task asks whether choreography helps identify a recurring musical phrase. That task serves [#187](https://github.com/rhythmatician/FractalSync/issues/187) and [#191](https://github.com/rhythmatician/FractalSync/issues/191). The synthetic first-hearing audio and assignment schedule are reproducible below. Julia exports and listener observations are pending.

## Primary evidence and limits

- Sievers, Polansky, Casey and Wheatley (2013) let participants express emotion by adjusting rate, timing jitter, direction, relative step size and dissonance or visual spikiness in monophonic music and an animated bouncing ball. Similar configurations occurred across the two modalities and in the US and Kreung samples, with some emotion-, modality- and sample-dependent differences. The generator established shared controls in advance. It gives testable dimensions for a new stimulus family, with no estimate of their effects in Julia motion. Laukka et al. (2013) found both cross-cultural recognition and culture-dependent cue use in expressive musical performances. Retain participant familiarity and avoid a universal dictionary of effects. [Sievers et al., PNAS](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1209023110), [Laukka et al., Emotion](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0031388).
- Laban Effort describes Time, Weight, Space and Flow. Shape describes spatial change, such as rising, sinking, enclosing or spreading. Shafir, Tsachor and Welch (2016) studied people performing movement motifs and rating their own evoked emotions. This motivates candidate contrasts between sudden and sustained, direct and indirect, or bound and released motion. Atkinson et al. (2004) varied the exaggeration of full-light and point-light body movement and found effects on recognition and intensity, with exceptions. Both experiments use bodily movement. Candidate measurements of a nonbodily image still need independent ratings. [Shafir et al., Frontiers in Psychology](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2015.02030), [Atkinson et al., Perception](https://doi.org/10.1068/p5096).
- Palmer et al. (2013) found that common affective associations accounted for a substantial part of music–color matching in their samples. Whiteford et al. (2018) found support for the association in 34 excerpts ranging across genres including heavy metal. These observations justify a later palette manipulation with fixed motion and motivate holding color fixed in the first trial. Kallinen and Ravaja (2006) found that rating the stimulus's expression versus one's own feeling could change music ratings. Ask those questions separately. [Palmer et al., PNAS](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1212562110), [Whiteford et al., i-Perception](https://doi.org/10.1177/2041669518808535), [Kallinen and Ravaja, Musicae Scientiae](https://doi.org/10.1177/102986490601000203).
- Fink, Fiehn and Wald-Fuhrmann (2024) found higher congruence for artist-intended visual art and music pairings than for shuffled pairings. The two audiovisual conditions did not significantly differ on their other subjective ratings or viewing/listening duration. Include a response with an objectively correct answer before asking about preference or synchronization. [Fink et al., Scientific Reports](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-71399-y).

## Candidate measurements

Use real time, source identity and validity masks for every measurement. Log the sampled values before assigning condition names. Phase, log amplitude and fractional rates require adequate mode amplitude and valid support. Keep transported heading within one gesture epoch. Resets, impulses, drift through rest and derivative failures interrupt smooth summaries. A CycleBank oscillator phase, a transported rider heading and a #139 probe link angle describe different events. If a probe endpoint supplies the rendered seed, identify its actual motion separately from the rider and link.

| Construct | Compact candidate | Competing explanation to control |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rate and timing regularity | Event count per second, median and median absolute deviation of inter-event intervals divided by the median. In audio use known onset times for synthetic clips. In visual trials retain intended action times and measured gesture peaks separately. | More events or more visible change can simply raise perceived activity. Equal rate does not imply matching phase or grouping. |
| Relative magnitude and Weight | Event speed integral and peak above local baseline. Compare robust spread and upper/lower event-size quantiles under matched total travel and peak speed where feasible. Audio uses accent amplitudes. Use intrinsic speed and kinetic energy for mechanical activity. | Perceived weight could come from easing, rendering or morphology. Metric energy and audio level remain separate predictors. |
| Direction, Space and Shape | Surging versus braking, positive versus negative covariant turn, and signed scale motion. Test turn imbalance and compare inward/outward ruler motion where gradients are valid. Measure observed expansion or enclosure from the rendered frames. | Absolute Map heading depends on presentation. Pitch ascent, screen ascent, motion toward the Shore and zoom have separate meanings. |
| Time, Flow and texture | Rise/settle duration, occupancy of slow and fast motion, smooth changes versus sharp changes in surge or carve, and motion fragmentation around stops. Use robust event summaries instead of differentiating noisy invalid samples. Record image-change and morphology summaries. | Musical dissonance, acoustic roughness, motion jerk and fractal surface appearance are distinct. Palette and glow can mimic an apparent motion change. |
| Log scale and phrasing | Compare reference-invariant log-speed and log-scale changes, relative onsets, recurrence timing and only justified phase relationships. Start with status and rates rather than absolute phase or position. | Similar time-scale algebra supplies candidate coordinates. It gives no perceptual equivalence or reason to map pitch to ruler scale. |

Use the currently recorded intrinsic speed, energy, scale and scale rate first. [#124](https://github.com/rhythmatician/FractalSync/issues/124) owns canonical transported phase, covariant surge/carve, turn rate, and curvature. Its proposed relation $\dot m/m=\dot u/u+i\Omega$ applies to a valid nonzero-speed epoch. The current snapshot's coordinate net acceleration is a separate quantity. [#123](https://github.com/rhythmatician/FractalSync/issues/123) owns the corresponding audio/visual mode algebra and [#126](https://github.com/rhythmatician/FractalSync/issues/126) the earlier phase-fit falsification design. [Rust snapshot definitions](../../runtime-core/src/debug.rs#L95).

At matched intrinsic speed, the #185 sampled Map and multiplier paths were close in one component, farther apart in another, and one Map contour refused. The exterior comparison uses distinct phase and equipotential coordinates. Choose two valid patches for the first silent stimulus block and report how image change depends on both path and patch. The [interior comparison](https://github.com/rhythmatician/FractalSync/blob/eb6f461d0335b049e2269f486f237581834b716d/docs/research/185-navigation-field-comparison.md), [exterior comparison](https://github.com/rhythmatician/FractalSync/blob/eb6f461d0335b049e2269f486f237581834b716d/docs/research/185-exterior-navigation.md), [#150](https://github.com/rhythmatician/FractalSync/issues/150) and [#140](https://github.com/rhythmatician/FractalSync/issues/140) address the separate geometry and appearance questions.

## Stimulus recipe

Use 480 canonical ticks at 1024 samples per tick and 48 kHz, a 10.24 s clip. Start each compared Controls replay at the same $c,v$ within its matched pair. Pilot candidate starts near $c=-0.10+0.10i$ and $c=-0.94+0.104i$ at rest, subject to the validity screen. Use bounded Controls-v2 pulses, with grip 0.5, brake 0.2 and zero impulse. During a pulse of $d$ ticks set throttle to $a\sin^2(\pi(h+1/2)/d)$ for tick $h=0,\ldots,d-1$. Between pulses set throttle to zero. Default headings cycle through the world unit directions $+x,+y,-x,-y$. Start with peak $a=0.18$. These are offline stimulus inputs for replay, not a live mapping.

**Silent motion pairs.** For each row, compare the two recipes with the same start, physics version and renderer. The ordinary pattern has eight 12-tick pulses at ticks $24+56k$, $k=0,\ldots,7$.

| Contrast | Reproducible pair |
| --- | --- |
| Rate | Six 16-tick pulses at $20+70k$ versus twelve 8-tick pulses at $20+35k$. Both commands have the same total throttle area. |
| Regularity | Ordinary timing versus adding $[0,12,-12,12,-12,12,-12,0]$ ticks to its starts. Keep pulse count, shapes and endpoints of the onset schedule. |
| Magnitude spread | Ordinary pulses versus alternating peaks of 0.09 and 0.27. Keep the mean and onset times. |
| Directional asymmetry | Eight pulses alternating $+x,-x$ versus the repeated pattern $+x,+x,+x,-x$. Log drift and scale occupancy. Confirm the contrast through measured motion rather than command direction alone. |
| Smoothness | Ordinary pulse envelope versus 12-tick flat pulses at half the peak, with the same onset and area. |
| Scale behavior | Use a fixed axis chosen from a valid start's Rust ruler gradient versus a fixed orthogonal axis. Alternate both axis signs with the ordinary timing. Screen for an actual contrast in scale excursion. |

**Phrase task.** Generate three consecutive 10-pulse phrases with a common pulse every 16 ticks. In family one, accents in pattern A occur at pulse offsets $[0,2,5,8]$ and in pattern B at $[0,3,5,8]$. In family two, use $[0,2,4,7]$ and $[0,2,5,7]$. Use versions AAB and ABA so the opening pattern returns in slot two or three, with the same pulse counts and interval multisets per pattern. For synthetic audio, add a 75 ms Hann-windowed sine at each shared pulse and a sine at gain 0.5 for each accent. Make five neutral pitch variants per family with fundamental frequencies 196, 220, 247, 262 and 294 Hz. Use twice the fundamental for family one's accent layer and three times for family two. Normalize audio assets to the same peak-safe RMS. Within a trial use the identical audio file for every audiovisual alternative. Hold out an entire family and its motion takes for a check against pilot-specific choices.

| Arm | Visual condition with the same audio |
| --- | --- |
| Matched | Four bounded 10-tick gestures per phrase, at the accent schedule for that audio. Each phrase uses the same four headings in the same order so the repeated timing organization can recur even as $c$ changes. |
| Same-activity mismatch | Use the other pattern order's frozen visual clip. Reuse both visuals as matched and mismatched across the two audio orders. |
| Timing negative | Shift the matched visual action starts eight ticks later, keeping the clip length, pulse shapes and count. The final pulse still ends inside the clip. Screen the shifted clip for motion and appearance differences caused by the delayed actions. |
| Shared-pulse baseline | One four-tick visual pulse at each shared pulse. Total active control ticks match the accents-only arm. The visual layer carries no phrase accent pattern. |
| Audio only | Calibrated audio with a blank field. During practice only, an explicitly legible mark stream can demonstrate the task, using separate examples. |

Choose a common fixed Julia viewport, camera, palette, exposure/glow, iteration and shader settings, resolution and frame rate. Reset shader time and use the same seed interpolation for every run. Any later palette/glow/zoom block uses the exact frozen trajectory and changes one presentation parameter within each pair. Screen the first block before collecting judgments. Reject clips with invalid snapshots, refusal, discontinuous capture or timing drift. Export the achieved event count, RMS intrinsic speed, total intrinsic travel, scale occupancy and simple image summaries such as foreground fraction and mean inter-frame luminance change. For audiovisual motion controls, target agreement within 10% on both RMS speed and total travel and check visible change separately. Tune only on pilot assets. If a silent manipulation also changes morphology or activity, retain the measured difference and label the contrast as mixed. A rejected or mismatched pair constrains what can be inferred.

## Blinded judgment and scoring

1. For a pilot, aim for 20 listeners in four blocks of five, including people outside the project. Record rhythmic training, familiarity and playback setup. With neutral media IDs and separate practice items, use ten first hearings per listener, five per family. Rotate the five arm assignments across each block so each item occurs once in every arm. Balance the two correct slots across blocks, shuffle presentation order and alternate which version appears first in each pair. Save the seed and final schedule, including any skipped or failed items. Mask the condition key in the participant view and during initial response coding.
2. Collect audio-only and silent-visual ratings independently on held-back examples. Rate perceived calm/agitation, heaviness/lightness, tension/release and regularity on separate seven-point items with labeled endpoints. Ask how the participant personally felt on a separate item and capture free-text reasons after scored questions. Leave room for ambiguous or mixed impressions.
3. For the phrase task, ask immediately after each first hearing which later phrase repeated the opening pattern. Score second/third choice against the authored answer and retain response latency and confidence. Assign just one arm per audio excerpt per participant for this first-exposure score. Use distinct excerpts for subsequent blinded A/B judgments, with the same audio in both halves. Ask which version made grouping clearer, which fit better and which was preferred as three questions. Collect synchronized-looking, visually active and felt-arousal ratings separately. Report first-hearing task scores and paired judgments separately since replay can teach the answer.
4. Freeze the primary contrast as first-hearing accuracy for matched versus same-activity mismatch. Estimate matched versus audio-only and shared-pulse baseline separately. Improvement over mismatch alone could indicate that mismatched motion hindered performance. A visual-only calibration on separate assets estimates how often the intended answer can be read from the visual cue alone. For transfer, use novel audio-only items after matched or control exposure and counterbalance which set appears first. Score transfer separately from immediate help.
5. Report condition counts and accuracy differences with participant-clustered intervals. A small mixed logistic model can add participant and excerpt intercepts with order and rhythmic familiarity as prespecified modifiers. Present simple paired differences or ordinal summaries for ratings. Compare an activity-only model with a small additional descriptor model on held-out song/trajectory families. Keep every variant of a source excerpt or take in one split. Use participants as units of uncertainty for the pilot and show individual disagreement. Freeze descriptors, masks and exclusions before unblinding. Treat results with sparse clips or broad intervals as exploratory.

If activity or appearance explains the expression differences, deprioritize motion-specific claims. If fit or preference rises without task accuracy, retain that narrower aesthetic finding. If there is a matched advantage over mismatch **and** audio-only on held-out items, continue the phrase study under #191. Check for a shared-pulse advantage and for clips that mislead listeners about the correct phrase. Expressive counterpoint can be preferred even when phase agreement is weaker. Any decision to keep or drop a descriptor should identify which result supported it and which alternatives remain. This pilot creates no new training target, observation channel or mapping.

## Generate the first-hearing inputs

~~~powershell
rtk proxy .\.venv\Scripts\python.exe formal/checks/prepare_expressive_stimuli.py
~~~

The [generator](../../formal/checks/prepare_expressive_stimuli.py) writes to ignored tmp/expressive-189/. The playback folder contains 20 original mono PCM WAVs and schedule.json, an ordered list of opaque trial and participant IDs with audio paths. The separate operator/key.json contains arm assignments, correct slots, file hashes, phrase boundaries and pulse and accent sample offsets. It also contains the proposed visual timing plans, including shifts and pulse widths, to compare later against applied Controls and rendered frames. Keep the operator folder out of the participant view. Use a private seed with a fresh output directory if the default production seed is known to participants.

Each WAV has 491,520 samples at 48 kHz with zero-based landmarks and an exclusive end, for 10.24 s. The generator checks the 12-accent schedule per excerpt and reopens the 16-bit PCM to check 30 shared pulse windows and silence between them. Post-quantization RMS was −26.020611 to −26.020594 dBFS, with peaks between −11.954 and −10.312 dBFS. This controls full-clip digital energy while leaving playback calibration and perceived loudness to the pilot preflight. The schedule contains 20 sessions and 200 first-hearing trials, with each arm used twice and each answer slot five times per listener. Two runs with the same source, seed and interpreter produced byte-identical WAVs and manifests.

The generated files contain audio inputs and timing plans. The pilot still needs recorded or replayed Controls, rendered Julia clips, independent rating and A/B assets, visual activity matching, and audiovisual synchronization checks. The audio schedule alone does not make any of the audiovisual arms ready to administer.
